# Filming batching plan

30 posts, but **only 9 of them need you to hold a phone**. Everything else is either
generated from MyPetLink or designed in Canva.

## The split

| Source | Posts | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🤖 Fully automatable from MyPetLink | 11, 15, 17, 19, 20, 21, 25, 26, 28 | ~2 hours at a desk, once |
| 🎨 Canva only (no camera, no app) | 7, 9, 23 | ~1.5 hours |
| 📱 Needs your phone | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 13, 14, 16, 18, 22, 24, 27, 29, 30 | 5 sessions, ~9 hours total |

The phone work collapses into **5 sessions** because the constraint is never the post —
it is the *setup*: where you are, what the pet is doing, and whether you are on camera.

---

## Session A — Talking head (2 hours, one afternoon)

**Covers posts 22, 24, 30** — and banks extra for future Q&A posts.

- **Setup once:** phone vertical on a stack of books at eye level, window on your face,
  plain wall behind, pet nearby if it cooperates. Wear the same top throughout so the
  three posts look like a series.
- **Shoot in this order** (easiest to hardest, so you warm up):
  1. Post 24 — why no GPS. Have a GPS collar and a printed paper tag in reach.
  2. Post 22 — who made this.
  3. Post 30 — first month. **Leave the numbers as a gap**; record that line again on
     posting day when you have the real figure.
- **Three takes each, no scripts.** Bullet points on a sticky note behind the phone.
- **Bank extra:** record 5–6 loose answers to likely questions. That is Post 26's video
  version and several weeks of future content, free.

**Output:** ~9 clips.

---

## Session B — Pet at home, calm (2 hours, golden hour)

**Covers posts 1, 5, 6, 18, 27, 29** — the emotional backbone of the account.

- **Setup:** tidy one corner of your home. Warm lamp plus window light. Get low, at the
  animal's eye level, for everything.
- **Shot list, in order:**
  - Post 1: hiding under furniture, untouched bowl, first step out, asleep on your foot
  - Post 5: dig the earliest adoption-day photos out of your camera roll instead of
    reshooting — authenticity wins here
  - Post 6: the five quirks. **These are opportunistic** — keep the phone handy all week
    and collect them rather than staging them
  - Post 18: pet with a small treat, birthday-ish warmth
  - Post 27: evening, lamp on, you photographing the pet, pet settling to sleep
  - Post 29: new-pet warmth, softer and brighter
- **Rule:** never force the animal. If it will not cooperate, move to the next shot and
  come back.

**Output:** ~25 stills, ~8 clips.

---

## Session C — Objects and flat-lays (1.5 hours, one table)

**Covers posts 2, 8, 13, 14** — the highest-save-rate posts on the account.

- **Setup:** one plain table, shoot straight down, natural window light, no flash.
- **Shot list:**
  - Post 2: four flat-lays — litter, food, vet receipt (**cover the clinic name and your
    details**), toys and carrier
  - Post 8: shoot both wrong and right versions of each photo rule. The wrong ones are
    the point — deliberately shoot too dark, from above, curled up, over-filtered
  - Post 13: moving boxes, a 「请勿开门 🐱」 sign taped to a door, pet in carrier
  - Post 14: hands checking a collar, a phone photographing a found pet, a group chat
    screenshot with **every name and number blurred**
- **Post 8 is the most valuable half-hour in this whole plan.** Do not rush it.

**Output:** ~20 stills.

---

## Session D — Phone-in-hand and scanning (1 hour)

**Covers posts 10, 12, 16** — the physical proof shots that make the product real.

- **You need two phones**: one displaying, one filming.
- **Prepare first:** print the pet's QR on paper (download from the Share Center), and
  have the Share Card open on the display phone.
- **Shot list:**
  - Post 12: filming phone captures the second phone's camera scanning the printed QR
    and the Safety Profile opening. **The moment the page appears is the shot.**
  - Post 16: same technique, scanning the QR inside the Share Card on screen
  - Post 10: the mess — a physical vaccine card in a drawer, a thumb scrolling a chaotic
    camera roll, a WhatsApp search
- **Watch for:** screen glare. Angle slightly off-perpendicular and kill overhead lights.

**Output:** ~6 clips.

---

## Session E — Out of the house (2.5 hours, one morning)

**Covers posts 3, 4** — the two hardest, so batch them into one trip.

- Post 4 needs **golden hour** at the kopitiam and the surau steps. Go early.
- **Ask permission before filming inside any shop.** No stranger's face in frame, ever.
- Post 3 needs an open gate at dusk plus the door-watching and window-grille shots, which
  you can pick up at home the same evening.
- **Never stage an actual escape** for Post 3. Film the signals, not the event.

**Output:** ~12 stills, ~4 clips.

## Batching rules that save the most time

1. **Batch by setup, never by post.** Moving the tripod and changing the light is the
   expensive part; pressing record is not.
2. **Shoot 3× what you need.** Extra clips become Reels, future posts, and replacements
   for the post that inevitably underperforms.
3. **Same outfit within a session.** Talking-head posts that look like a series get
   followed as a series.
4. **Do the automated run last.** By then you will know which screenshots you actually
   need, instead of guessing.
5. **Never film with a stressed animal.** Reschedule. A reluctant pet reads as a
   reluctant pet on camera, and this audience notices immediately.
